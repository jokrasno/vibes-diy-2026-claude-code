Yes — that’s **much stronger** than “one photo → one mini-game.”

The grander version is:

> **A photo-based RPG engine where every room, encounter, item, enemy, and quest is generated from photos or video you capture.**

That feels like a real product: **“turn your world into an RPG campaign.”**

## Best framing

Call it something like:

**RealityRPG**
**PocketQuest**
**CameraQuest**
**PhotoRPG**
**Worlds From Your Camera**

Core pitch:

> Take photos of your room, campus, desk, car, backyard, or neighborhood. The app converts each place into a pixel-art RPG level, turning real objects into enemies, NPCs, items, quests, shops, bosses, and portals to the next area.

# Recommended version for a 5-hour hackathon

## MVP: **Photo-RPG Dungeon Crawler**

Use multiple photos as multiple RPG rooms.

```text
Photo 1 → Level 1: Bedroom Village
Photo 2 → Level 2: Desk Dungeon
Photo 3 → Level 3: Kitchen Cavern
Photo 4 → Boss Room: Backyard Arena
```

Each photo becomes a level with:

```text
pixel-art background
player start
NPC / enemy / collectible objects
quest objective
exit portal to next photo-level
```

The player moves through a chain of generated levels.

That is way more compelling than a single-level arcade game.

```text

  ↓
AI converts scene into pixel-RPG level
  ↓

  ↓

  ↓
Exit portal opens
  ↓
Next photo becomes next level
```

Example:


# Why this is better

A single photo game says:

> “Cool, it made a level.”

A photo-RPG says:

> “This is an engine that turns my real world into an adventure.”

That is much more “grand.”

It also gives you a strong demo story:

> “We took three random photos around the room, and the system built a connected RPG campaign out of them.”

# Best 5-hour architecture

Keep the engine fixed. The AI generates **level JSON**, not arbitrary code.

```text
photos/video frames
  ↓
scene analyzer
  ↓
world/campaign generator
  ↓
level JSON files
  ↓
fixed Phaser RPG runtime
```

## Main runtime

Use a simple top-down pixel RPG engine:

```text
WASD movement
NPC dialogue boxes
collectible quest items
enemies/hazards
portals between levels
inventory
health
final boss
```

Do **not** implement complex RPG combat. Use collision/avoidance or simple bump combat.

## Stage 2 — Scene analysis per photo

Each photo becomes:

```json
{
  "scene_id": "desk",
  "scene_summary": "A desk with a laptop, mug, notebook, headphones, and cables.",
  "objects": [
    {
      "name": "laptop",
      "bbox": [0.42, 0.15, 0.77, 0.42],
      "suggested_role": "portal"
    },
    {
      "name": "coffee mug",
      "bbox": [0.08, 0.38, 0.24, 0.58],
      "suggested_role": "enemy"
    },
    {
      "name": "notebook",
      "bbox": [0.62, 0.62, 0.91, 0.88],
      "suggested_role": "quest_item"
    }
  ]
}
```

---

## Stage 3 — Campaign generation

After analyzing all photos, send all scene summaries to the LLM and ask it to create:

```json
{
  "campaign_title": "The Curse of Deskoria",
  "story_premise": "The real world has been transformed into a tiny pixel RPG kingdom.",
  "levels": [
    {
      "level_id": "bedroom_village",
      "source_photo": "photo_01",
      "role": "starter_village"
    },
    {
      "level_id": "desk_dungeon",
      "source_photo": "photo_02",
      "role": "first_dungeon"
    },
    {
      "level_id": "kitchen_cavern",
      "source_photo": "photo_03",
      "role": "challenge_room"
    },
    {
      "level_id": "backyard_boss_arena",
      "source_photo": "photo_04",
      "role": "boss_room"

  "global_quest": {
    "goal": "Collect three lost relics and defeat the Coffee Slime King.",

```

This makes it feel like a real RPG campaign.
